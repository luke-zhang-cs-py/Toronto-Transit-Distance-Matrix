Toronto-Transit-Distance-Matrix · port 5000

Which stops you can reach, and when you would get there

A local Flask app that answers three questions about getting around Toronto: how far you can get in a given time, how to make a particular trip, and what time you will actually arrive. Every travel time is computed here, against the TTC's own timetable and its live service feed. No API key and no billing account — both feeds are open, and the map tiles are plain OpenStreetMap.

Repository Open app (local)

Where each number comes from, and how much to trust it

Three different kinds of number appear on the same screen and they do not deserve equal confidence, so every wait carries its source and the page shows it. This is the app's one real opinion.

The source is on every leg of every trip, not a global setting.

Two things worth knowing about the routing

  

    

      
A wait is charged for every boarding

      

        routing.py charges one wait, at the origin, and its
        transfer edges cost a flat three minutes for the platform walk with no
        wait for the next vehicle — so a trip with two changes undercounted by
        two waits. itinerary.py fixes that, and fixing it forces
        the search state: riding a train through a station has to be
        free while changing lines there is not, so a state is
        (stop, line you are riding) rather than just a stop.
      

    

    

      
Leaving later cannot get you there earlier

      

        Transit is FIFO, and that property is what lets Dijkstra work on a
        clock rather than on fixed weights. It is not an assumption left
        implicit —
        test_leaving_later_never_arrives_earlier asserts it, and
        if it ever failed the time-dependent search would be unsound rather
        than merely wrong.
      

    

  







  
The timetable index, and why it expires

  

    tools_build_schedule.py streams the TTC's 207 MB GTFS
    archive once and writes a small index of departures. Everything works
    without it, on live headways and modelled waits, and every response says
    which it used.
